\section{Approach}

Predict a photo's GPS coordinate (12 European countries, $11{,}758$ labelled
images, $2{,}400$ held out) with \emph{one} ${\le}5{,}000{,}000$-parameter
model, no pretrained weights, no external data.

We predict by \textbf{retrieval}: embed query and bank, copy the nearest
match's coordinate. Retrieval is not continuous --- its output is restricted to
the $11{,}758$ training coordinates --- but that discretisation is cheap: the
geographically nearest training photo is a median $3.1$\,km away and within
$50$\,km for $99.8\%$ of queries, far below the error we achieve, whereas a
geo-cell classifier is bounded by its cell size. The gain is that the encoder
never regresses a coordinate; it only learns which photos were taken near each
other, which supervises densely from every pair in a batch.

\section{Model (Fig.~\ref{fig:arch})}

One RegNetY-400MF trunk~\cite{regnet} (\texttt{weights=None}) is shared across
three views: the full frame at $512^2$ and aligned left/right crops at $256^2$,
which keep detail a single downscaled frame destroys.

\textbf{Global branch.} Multi-scale GeM pooling~\cite{gem} over stages 2--4,
fused across views, through an identity-initialised $384\!\times\!384$ layer,
L2-normalised to a descriptor $g$.
\textbf{Local branch.} Stage-4 features average-pooled to $4\!\times\!4$ per
view (48 tokens), projected $440\!\to\!64$, L2-normalised to tokens $T$.

The shortlist is the cosine top-200 of $\langle g_q,g_b\rangle$, rescored by a
symmetric MaxSim late interaction~\cite{colbert}:
\[
s(q,b)=0.2\,\langle g_q,g_b\rangle+0.8\cdot\tfrac12\!\left(
\overline{\max_j}\,T_qT_b^{\!\top}+\overline{\max_i}\,T_qT_b^{\!\top}\right)
\]
The top-1 candidate's coordinate is the prediction.

\section{Training}\label{sec:train}

Evaluated on the official 5-fold split: train on four folds, predict the fifth,
fixed epoch count, no checkpoint selection.

\textbf{(1) BYOL pretraining}~\cite{byol} of the trunk, labels unused, 160
epochs. \textbf{(2) Country-aware retrieval fine-tuning}, 40 epochs, AdamW
($8{\times}10^{-5}$ trunk, $6{\times}10^{-4}$ heads), physical batch 32 with 2
gradient-accumulation steps (effective 64), EMA $0.999$. Each group is an
anchor, two geographic positives and one \emph{same-country} hard negative
$60$--$700$\,km away, re-mined from the current EMA encoder every epoch.
Positives are the 8 most descriptor-similar of the 64 geographically nearest
that lie within 50\,km; for the $10.7\%$ of anchors with fewer than 8 such
neighbours the sampler falls back to the 8 nearest \emph{regardless of
distance}, so ``$\le$50\,km'' holds for $\approx89\%$ of anchors, not all.
Anchors from the weakest countries are oversampled (DE $3.0\times$, FR $2.8$,
PL $2.0$, IT/ES $1.7$, SE $1.4$, GB $1.3$). Objective: $0.65$ retrieval $+\,0.2$
classification regulariser.

so we separate them on fold 0: baseline (no BYOL, no country-aware, ep 6)

advance.

\section{What did not work}

\textbf{Decode-time repairs on frozen descriptors (5-fold evidence).} A learned
candidate reranker was tried four times (linear, residual, pairwise, and one
gated on a held-out inner fold) and degraded the crossfit every time; the
inner-fold gate passed only because the probe fold was held out from the
reranker but not from the \emph{encoder}. Kernel-density consensus decoding
gave 72\,km against 57.7. Gating the shortlist on predicted country cost 4\,km
pooled (country accuracy is 68\%, and a wrong gate is unrecoverable). Decoding
from the auxiliary geo-cell head instead of retrieval gave DE 490 / FR 523\,km
against retrieval's 534 / 551: two decoders reading the same descriptor fail
equally, which locates the problem upstream of the decoder --- though it does
not establish what the descriptor is missing.

\textbf{Encoder variants (fold 0 only --- weaker evidence).} Adding a
random-resized-crop to the global view was worse at every matched epoch
(63.7 vs 58.1\,km at epoch 32), consistent enough to call a backfire; it
discards the horizon and scene layout. Raising the local grid from
$4\!\times\!4$ to $8\!\times\!8$ was \emph{not} worse (58.3 vs 60.7\,km at
epoch 40) --- we kept $4\!\times\!4$ as the simpler option, not because the
variant failed; extending it to 48 epochs drifted to 61.8\,km. Interpret both
cautiously: the shipped recipe's own fold-0 median varies by $5.3$\,km across
epochs 20--40, so single-fold gaps of that size are not decisive.

\section{Limitations}

Germany and France are the dominant failure and we did not solve them. The
evidence locates the problem in the representation rather than the decoder, but
we never ran the experiment that would say \emph{why} --- e.g.\ probing whether
the descriptor carries any within-country position signal. Country-aware
oversampling targeted exactly this and moved every other weak country but not
these two. Also: the failed-variant evidence is single-fold and confounded by
epoch count, and the figures are rebuilt from cached out-of-fold predictions
that are not in the repository, so they cannot be regenerated from a clean
clone.

\section{Submission}

The recipe retrained once on all $11{,}758$ images with no held-out fold, from
an existing BYOL trunk, using the configuration's default seed:
$4{,}869{,}911$ parameters, \textbf{67\,min of fine-tuning} on one RTX 4000 Ada
(excluding BYOL pretraining, ${\approx}2$\,h\,40\,min, which was reused rather
than re-run), plus ${\approx}5$\,min to write \texttt{predictions.csv}.
