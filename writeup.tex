\section{Problem}

Predict the GPS coordinate of a photo from twelve European countries, scored by
median haversine error. The rules allow \emph{one} model of at most
$5{,}000{,}000$ parameters, no pretrained weights, and no data beyond the
$11{,}758$ labelled training images.

Regression and grid classification both quantise the answer, so the model treats
geolocation as \textbf{retrieval}: embed the query and every training image into a
common space, take the nearest training images, and read the coordinate of the
best match. This yields a continuous prediction from a compact encoder.

\section{Architecture (Fig.~\ref{fig:arch})}

A single RegNetY-400MF trunk (\texttt{weights=None}) is shared across three views:
the full frame at $512^2$ and aligned left/right crops at $256^2$, which retain
high-resolution detail a single downscaled frame loses. Two branches read it:

\textbf{Global.} Multi-scale GeM pooling over stages 2--4, fused across views,
passed through an identity-initialised $384\!\times\!384$ layer and
L2-normalised to a 384-D descriptor. Cosine similarity to the bank gives a
top-200 shortlist.

\textbf{Local.} Stage-4 features average-pooled to a $4\!\times\!4$ grid per view
(48 tokens), projected $440\!\to\!64$, L2-normalised. A bidirectional MaxSim
(ColBERT-style late interaction) reranks the shortlist.

Final score $=0.2\,\text{global}+0.8\,\text{local}$; the top-1 candidate's
coordinate is the prediction. Country and geo-cell heads on the fused descriptor
act only as a training regulariser.

\section{Training}

Two stages, evaluated on the official 5-fold split (train four, predict the
fifth, fixed epochs, no checkpoint selection).

\textbf{1. Self-supervised pretraining.} BYOL (negative-free, momentum target and
predictor), labels unused, 160 epochs. A trunk warm-started only by the
classification signal never learns features that separate locations \emph{within}
a large, visually uniform country; BYOL gives it a sharper starting
representation.

\textbf{2. Country-aware retrieval fine-tuning}, 40 epochs from the BYOL trunk.
The loss combines spatial-listwise, local-listwise, triplet and
local-verification terms. Each group is an anchor, two geographic positives
($\le 35$ km) and one \emph{same-country} hard negative mined from the current
EMA encoder and refreshed every epoch, forcing the encoder to separate nearby
same-country scenes. Anchors from the weakest countries are oversampled.

\section{Germany and France: unresolved}

Every other country's median is under 220 km; Germany (505) and France (570) do
not move. This is a representation gap, confirmed three ways: retrieval ranking,
retrieval recall (DE/FR oracle@200 plateaus near 60\% vs.\ 80\% pooled), and the
geo-cell head, which is equally lost there. Country-aware oversampling and
same-country hard negatives target exactly this and barely helped.

\begin{figure}[h]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.98\linewidth]{images/figures/error_map.png}
\caption{Misses (lines) are concentrated in Germany/France (red), not spread.}
\label{fig:map}
\end{figure}

German and French street scenes---suburban architecture, road furniture,
vegetation---repeat across hundreds of kilometres, while Nordic and Icelandic
scenes carry distinctive terrain. A $\le$5M-parameter from-scratch encoder at
512 px lacks the capacity to read the fine localised cues (signage, plates,
micro-architecture) that separate them. That is the clearest direction for a
larger budget.

\section{Submission}

The submitted model retrains the recipe once on all $11{,}758$ labelled images
(no held-out fold), from an existing BYOL trunk: $4{,}869{,}911$ parameters,
$\approx$70 min on one RTX 4000 Ada, plus $\approx$5 min to write
\texttt{predictions.csv} ($2{,}400$ rows) via the top-200 shortlist and
late-interaction rerank.
